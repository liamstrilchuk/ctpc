Our Coding Platform

A look at what competing in a CTPC coding competition felt like — plus a small, self-contained demo you can try right now.

The participant journey

Here's how our custom competition platform worked for past CTPC events. These are real features of the platform — not the demo below.

1. Registering

Students could register individually, or join through their school once a teacher registered using a school code. Teams were formed within a school for the team portion of the competition.

2. Reading a problem

Each problem had a written description along with sample inputs and expected outputs, so participants could check their understanding before submitting.

3. Writing and testing code

Participants wrote code directly in the browser, in Python, C++, Java, C, or JavaScript, with syntax highlighting. Before submitting for real, they could run practice submissions against the sample test cases to check their solution.

4. Submitting and getting graded

Each submission was automatically checked against the problem's test cases and given a result, such as Accepted, Wrong Answer, Time Limit Exceeded, or Runtime Error.

5. Teams and the leaderboard

In the team portion, a team's combined results counted toward its standing, and a live leaderboard showed how teams and individuals were doing across the contest. Our 2025 format ran a one-hour individual round and a two-hour team round, with in-person and virtual participants competing the same day.
